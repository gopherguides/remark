# Header One

**Since** its inception around the beginning of the twentieth century, the dedicated music recording and production facility has been increasingly designed as a highly specialized space. Similar to the development of the concert hall over the centuries, the music production facility has gradually moved from ad hoc, to being designed by intuition for the purpose of solving the unique problems of the task, to being designed through the application of scientific principles, and finally to highly scientifically-aided technology-driven design informed by practice and iteration. So, what are the elements that go into making a successful music recording and/or production facility?

A studio specializing in machine-driven pop, EDM, or hip-hop, on the other hand, will likely require a smaller physical footprint, with perhaps just a booth for recording individual instruments or vocals. At the same time, the SPLs and low frequency content involved may dictate not only high-power extended frequency response monitoring, but a high level of sound isolation, depending in part on the studio's surroundings.
